# Full-manuscript consistency

Use this reference for a full-manuscript revision, proofreading pass, final
submission check, or a manuscript changed across multiple rounds. A local
paragraph edit cannot establish consistency across the paper, so sweep the
supplied manuscript scope before treating the prose as final.

## Scope

Check every supplied representation of the study:

- title, abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, and Conclusions;
- figure and table captions, labels, legends, and cross-references;
- supplementary text, tables, and figures when they are in scope;
- sample, object, group, stage, experiment, and dataset names;
- numerical results, units, significant figures, statistical terms, symbols,
  and formulas.

Use the terminology ledger from
[terminology-and-consistency.md](terminology-and-consistency.md) as the
terminology source of truth. This sweep adds cross-document checks; it does not
replace close reading of the evidence or the terminology contexts.

## Sweep in passes

### 1. Identity and labels

Check that the same sample, object, group, stage, experiment, variable, or
dataset keeps the same name and identifier. Check figure, table, equation, and
supplementary numbering against the supplied files. Flag a mismatch even when
the surrounding prose is grammatically correct.

### 2. Numbers, units, and precision

Reconcile repeated values across the abstract, Results, Discussion,
Conclusions, figures, and tables. Check that:

- the same quantity is not reported in conflicting units;
- repeated values use a defensible and stable precision;
- percentages, totals, sample counts, ranges, and averages agree;
- statistical labels match the quantity being reported;
- conversions are numerically correct when different units are intentional.

The same number can represent different quantities. Inspect its label, unit,
population, time, and condition before treating a repeated value as a conflict.

### 3. Findings and claims

Compare every headline finding and conclusion with the result or display that
supports it. Check for:

- an abstract or Conclusion that adds a result absent from the main text;
- a claim of highest, lowest, increased, decreased, or significant when the
  table or figure shows an exception or does not support that strength;
- a Discussion interpretation that reverses, broadens, or changes the scope of
  the Results;
- an important finding present in Results but silently lost from the abstract
  or Conclusion;
- a repeated claim that credits different variables, samples, mechanisms, or
  evidence in different sections.

Keep observation, interpretation, and implication distinct while comparing
these sections. Repeated wording may change with section function, but the
underlying scientific meaning and scope must remain compatible.

### 4. Cross-references and displays

Verify that each in-text reference points to the intended figure, table,
equation, section, or supplementary item, and that the cited display actually
contains the feature described. Check captions against the plotted or tabulated
content. Do not infer a display's value from a caption alone when the underlying
figure or table is available.

### 5. Conditions and scope

Check that a result is not silently transferred across different samples,
conditions, time points, spatial scales, or analytical methods. A statement can
be numerically identical yet scientifically inconsistent if its population or
conditions changed. Record those scope differences beside the finding.

## Report before changing

Record each finding with its locations, the two or more statements or displays
being compared, the type of mismatch, and its likely effect. Separate:

- a clear mechanical inconsistency, which may be corrected directly and listed
  in the change summary;
- a discrepancy whose cause is unknown, which requires source or author
  checking;
- a conflict that could change the scientific interpretation, which must be
  reported to the author before revision.

Do not make one location agree with another by guessing which is correct. Check
the underlying data, figure, table, source, or author decision first. When the
source of truth is unavailable, mark the item as unresolved and pause only the
affected claim or display.

## Completion check

Before delivery, confirm that repeated findings agree across the supplied
manuscript scope, every display reference resolves, and every unresolved
discrepancy is surfaced to the author. After a content correction, repeat the
affected passes because changing a value, claim, or label can expose a new
inconsistency elsewhere.
