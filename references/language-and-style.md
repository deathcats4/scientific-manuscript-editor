# Language and style

## Shared target

Write clear, restrained, information-dense academic prose. Keep technical terms
stable; repetition of a correct term is preferable to decorative synonyms.
Prefer concrete subjects and results over abstract nouns and generic evaluation.

## Remove low-value prose

Replace or remove empty significance claims, formulaic transitions, repeated
conclusions, and explanations about the act of writing. Use a connector only
when it expresses a real contrast, comparison, consequence, or continuation.

Present the claim and its evidence in the order most useful to the reader. Keep
qualification beside the claim it actually qualifies. A qualification belongs
in the main text when deleting it would make the claim materially stronger,
broader, more causal, or applicable under different conditions; otherwise
compress or remove it. Technical detail can move to the appropriate caption or
Methods context when it does not change the reader's interpretation.

## Defensive phrasing

Prefer a direct, evidence-linked statement over a sequence of assurances,
imagined rebuttals, or generic warnings. Do not manufacture a stronger claim in
order to rebut it. Preserve a limitation or negative result when it changes the
meaning or scope of the central conclusion. A different model becomes a
manuscript issue only through the author decision gate in the main skill; do not
add it merely because a cited paper uses a different explanation.

For any proposed caveat, apply the claim-first evidential discipline in
[evidence-and-citations.md](evidence-and-citations.md): first make the claim
itself accurate in subject, range, causality, and certainty. Keep a separate
limitation only when that calibrated claim would still materially mislead the
reader without it. At the same time, retain the inferential steps needed to show
why the evidence supports the interpretation. Confidence comes from a complete
argument with bounded claims, not from either unexplained leaps or repeated
self-protection.

## English and Chinese–English transfer

Use common journal English, natural syntax, and manageable sentence length. Do
not add meaning for the sake of sounding native. For Chinese source text, first
resolve the scientific function and modality, then reorganize into the target
language; flag ambiguity when a conservative translation cannot choose safely.
