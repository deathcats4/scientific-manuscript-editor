# Results

Use for Results drafting, evidence organization, diagnosis, or substantive
restructuring. For a local language edit, preserve a sound existing evidence
architecture.

## Scientific function

Results should make the empirical case legible: what was found, under which
conditions, by which evidence, with what strength or uncertainty, and how the
findings answer the study's scientific questions.

Figures and tables are evidence containers, not an automatic outline. The order
of experiments, analyses, or figure numbers is useful only when it also matches
the scientific logic.

## Determine the evidence relationship

Identify the anchor finding and the role of other results. They may provide a
comparison, control, discriminating test, convergent evidence, validation,
robustness, boundary, null result, scale transition, component analysis, or a
parallel answer to another question.

Then organize around the relationship actually present—for example:

- question → evidence → bounded answer;
- central finding → quantitative support and controls;
- progressive evidence that increases confidence;
- comparison between meaningful conditions;
- convergence of different measurements or scales;
- process or temporal progression;
- robustness, heterogeneity, and domain boundaries;
- several parallel questions that each have their own evidence chain.

These are possible movements, not mandatory templates.

## Evidence and interpretation

Report enough empirical detail for the reader to trace each important finding
to its support. Extract the meaningful comparison or trend instead of
transcribing every visible value. Include controls, negative results,
heterogeneity, or failure cases when they affect evaluation of the claim.

Use limited local interpretation when the reader needs it to understand what a
result establishes. In a conventional separate Results section, reserve broader
mechanism, theory, literature positioning, and field-level implications for
Discussion. In a combined Results and Discussion section, follow the passage's
actual function.

## Guardrails

- Give each result the weight its evidence and role require.
- Link every important observation to a scientific role.
- End each subsection with a claim whose supporting observation or comparison is
  visible.
- Interpret p-values, model scores, and threshold crossings through the full
  evidence rather than treating them as importance or mechanism by themselves.
- Report null and non-significant results with the sensitivity and uncertainty of
  the analysis; attribute non-detection only when the analysis supports it.
- Keep parallel findings parallel when they answer distinct questions.
- Keep meaningful boundaries visible even when they qualify the headline.

## Completion check

The reader should be able to see what was established, which evidence supports
it, under what conditions it applies, and why the order of findings is
scientifically coherent.
