# Manuscript reasoning

Use this reference for substantive paragraph or section work. Its purpose is to
recover the science that the prose must convey, not to impose a visible
framework on the author.

## Build the minimum useful scientific model

Determine:

1. **Reader endpoint** — what should the intended reader understand or accept
   after this passage?
2. **Evidence basis** — which supplied observations, results, analyses, or
   sources support that endpoint?
3. **Necessary inference** — what non-obvious relationship connects the
   evidence to the claim?
4. **Material boundary** — which condition, uncertainty, or domain limit would
   change the meaning if omitted?
5. **Author state** — which interpretation is settled, which is exploratory,
   and which choice remains open?

Use only the elements the task needs. Some papers have one central claim;
others make several connected contributions. Do not force false unity merely
to make the narrative look simple.

## Diagnose the real writing problem

Before choosing an organization, identify what currently prevents the intended
reader from reaching the endpoint. Common causes include:

- the scientific question is not yet visible;
- evidence is present but its role is unclear;
- a necessary inference is missing;
- several findings need synthesis rather than sequential reporting;
- literature is listed without a relationship to the current claim;
- a boundary is hidden or a generic caveat obscures a narrower valid claim;
- the section answers a different question from the one it raises.

Repair that problem rather than applying a preferred template.

## Choose movement from scientific relationships

Organize the passage around the relationship that matters: progression,
comparison, convergence, discrimination between explanations, scale
transition, process, boundary, anomaly, or another relationship present in the
material. Familiar structures such as funnel, CARS, problem–solution,
hypothesis-driven, claim-first, or evidence-first are optional tools. Combine,
adapt, reverse, or ignore them when the science calls for another movement.

Structure is not a fixed sentence sequence. A paragraph may begin with a
question, judgment, observation, prior study, anomaly, or boundary. What matters
is that each sentence performs work needed for the paragraph's scientific
function and that the endpoint prepares the reader for what follows.

## Reasoning density

Do not confuse concision with omission. State an inferential step when an expert
reader could not reliably supply it from disciplinary knowledge and the
available context. Omit internal deliberation, obvious background, and
alternatives that do not materially affect the active claim.

When several evidence streams matter jointly, explain what their convergence
adds and what each stream can establish. When they answer parallel questions,
allow parallel contributions rather than inventing one linear mechanism.

## Author-approved science, editorial freedom

Inside settled scientific boundaries, choose wording, paragraph progression,
emphasis, and organization directly. If a proposed organization changes which
evidence bears on which claim, changes the scientific emphasis or scope, or
commits the paper to a new interpretation, present it as a recommendation until
the author accepts it.

## Final reasoning check

Ask only:

- Is the reader endpoint clear?
- Can the central claim be traced to supplied support?
- Is every necessary non-obvious inference recoverable?
- Is the claim bounded at the level the evidence earns?
- Does the prose express the current author position rather than revision
  history or the editor's private reasoning?
