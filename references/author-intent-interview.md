# Author-intent interview

Use this reference when a manuscript request is unclear, substantive, or could
change the author's scientific position. The goal is shared understanding of
the work to perform, not permission for ordinary editorial choices.

## Decision tree

Work through the frontier of currently answerable decisions. The usual nodes
are:

1. **Operation** — local edit, translation, logic/evidence review, drafting,
   restructuring, citation review, naturalness review, or manuscript-wide
   synthesis.
2. **Scope** — sentence, paragraph, section, selected sections, or full
   manuscript; identify the intended reader endpoint when section scale is
   involved.
3. **Author state** — which claims, mechanisms, causal relations, contribution
   framing, terminology, and conclusion strength are accepted, rejected, or
   provisional.
4. **Evidence basis** — which observations, figures, tables, methods,
   limitations, and inspected sources support the requested move; mark any
   load-bearing gap.
5. **Source role and authorization** — whether a supplied paper is being used
   for facts, citation support, terminology, reasoning, comparison, or style;
   ask separately whether new literature retrieval is authorized.
6. **Delivery** — manuscript-only text, findings before rewriting, a proposed
   organization, a provisional draft, or progressive paragraph-by-paragraph
   collaboration.

Do not ask every node by default. Start with the earliest unresolved node whose
answer changes the downstream route. If several frontier questions are
independent, ask them in one round. If a later question depends on an earlier
answer, defer it to the next round.

## Round format

Use a compact format with a recommended answer. Recommendations are proposals,
not author commitments:

```text
❓ Q1 — 处理目标：你希望我只改语言，还是同时检查这段解释是否被结果支持？

➡️ 建议：先做科学逻辑与证据边界审查，因为当前请求涉及机制解释。
```

After the author answers, update the working state and ask only the new
frontier. Do not repeat settled decisions or turn the interview into a list of
questions whose answers are already present in the manuscript.

## Facts versus decisions

Facts are the agent's job. Read the supplied text, figures, tables, methods,
notes, and accessible source material before asking the author to restate them.
The author's decisions are the author's job. Ask when the material does not
settle whether a mechanism is accepted, whether a comparison belongs in the
paper, whether a claim may become stronger, or what role a supplied reference
should play.

If a load-bearing fact or source is missing, report the concrete gap and why it
changes the requested result. Do not use a plausible default to close it. The
author may supply the material, authorize retrieval or analysis, or accept a
visibly provisional draft with the gap marked.

## Completion

The interview is complete when the operation, scope, relevant author state,
evidence basis, source authorization, and delivery form are either settled or
explicitly marked as open constraints. Return to the main skill, apply the
selected depth and references, and keep unresolved scientific choices outside
the manuscript until the author accepts them.

## Example: Discussion mechanism

User: “帮我把这段 Discussion 写得更有说服力。”

First round:

```text
❓ Q1 — 说服力的目标：你要增强语言表达，还是增强从结果到机制的论证？

➡️ 建议：先检查证据到机制的推理，因为单纯增强语气可能改变论断强度。

❓ Q2 — 当前立场：这个机制是你已经接受的解释，还是需要我比较的候选方案？

➡️ 建议：先把它标为“与结果一致的候选解释”，除非你的数据已经支持更强的判断。
```

If the author accepts the recommendation, the next round can ask whether a
comparison mechanism belongs in the paper and whether literature retrieval is
authorized. If the supplied results do not include the boundary condition that
the explanation depends on, return a material-sufficiency gap instead of
writing the missing condition from memory.
