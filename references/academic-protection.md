# Academic expression protection

Use for AI-style review, academic naturalness revision, or an edit that risks
removing a meaningful scholarly form. This is a functional protection guide,
not a vocabulary list. It applies across disciplines in Chinese and English
without requiring a training corpus or a user-built list.

## Protect meaning before changing form

The entrypoint's integrity boundary and author control govern every edit.
Within them, distinguish three kinds of protection:

| Kind | What to protect | Editing consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Exact content | Numbers, units, identifiers, formulas, statistical notation, citation keys, quotations, and text explicitly requested verbatim | Preserve during naturalness work. Raise suspected errors separately; correct only within an authorized correction task. |
| Scholarly function | Uncertainty, scope, attribution, comparison, negation, sequence, methodological focus, and reasoning links | Wording may change when the same function and strength survive. |
| Local convention | Defined terms, declared aliases, established notation, and intentional rhetorical choices | Use the current manuscript and supplied context. Retain distinctions; treat similar terms as interchangeable only when the context establishes that equivalence. |

These protections preserve expression while claim and citation verification
remains a separate task. Missing context calls for calibrated handling of the
available evidence.

## Functional protections, not word exemptions

| Form encountered | Preserve when | Repair when |
|---|---|---|
| Modals and qualifiers, such as `may`, `suggests`, `可能`, `与……一致` | They express the actual distance between evidence and interpretation or a necessary scope limit | Qualifiers repeat the same uncertainty without adding meaning. Distinct uncertainties can legitimately coexist. |
| Assertive wording, such as `demonstrates`, `establishes`, `表明` | Its strength matches the supplied basis and accepted author position | A material mismatch exists; handle under scientific integrity, not a banned-verb rule. |
| Passive voice and nominalization | They keep attention on a procedure, object, defined construct, or relationship | They hide a needed actor or relation, or add avoidable abstraction. Choose voice by function; active voice has no quota. |
| Connectives and contrasts | They express a real addition, consequence, concession, distinction, or comparison | They imply a relation the content does not establish, or repeatedly announce a relation already clear. Preserve both claims when recasting. |
| Repetition and parallel structure | Stable terminology, comparable results, parallel evidence, or ordered steps need it | Several sentences repeat the same proposition without additional evidence, inference, boundary, or navigational need. |
| First person, impersonal prose, and rhetorical emphasis | They fit the manuscript's section, discipline, and authorized style basis | They conflict with that context. Let the section and style basis determine the academic voice. |
| Chinese scholarly forms, such as `性`, `化`, `进行`, `基于` | They name a concept or express a useful scholarly relation | The particular phrase adds empty packaging; a suffix or verb alone is not a defect. |

Examples in this table identify functions rather than supply phrases. During
revision, preserve the function already present. During new drafting or
translation, choose forms warranted by the supplied claims and target language.
Review added qualifiers, connectors, and passive constructions for a concrete
academic function.

Evidence may be numerical, formal, textual, archival, qualitative, or another
discipline-appropriate form. Match each theoretical, empirical, and interpretive
claim to the evidence it has.

## Resolve a suspected false positive

Ask what would be lost if the expression disappeared. Preserve its factual or
logical contribution before deciding whether its surface form needs repair.
Check the local section and style basis using the existing style-source rules.
Routine edits use the supplied manuscript context directly. When a convention is
unfamiliar and the defect remains uncertain, retain it or flag the uncertainty
outside the manuscript; apply a correction as a local manuscript decision.

## Boundary examples

These are constructed editing examples, not findings to reuse in a manuscript.

- **Calibrated inference:** `These observations may indicate a change in X.`
  Preserve `may indicate`; changing it to `prove` changes the claim.
- **Supported certainty:** `Theorem 1 establishes uniqueness under assumption A.`
  Preserve the asserted result and its assumption when supported; retain its
  direct strength rather than adding `may` merely because the sentence is
  assertive.
- **Method focus:** `Responses were coded independently by two reviewers.`
  The passive is functional; preserve the independence and reviewer count.
- **Real contrast:** `X increased, whereas Y remained unchanged.` Preserve both
  outcomes and their contrast, even when other results use the same structure.
- **Terminology:** Repeated `social capital` or `社会资本` may maintain one
  defined construct. Decorative synonyms can change that construct.
- **Translation:** Translating `结果可能反映 X` may require `may reflect X`.
  Translate the functional meaning and scholarly register rather than a literal
  word sequence.
- **Quoted material:** A study may quote `I hope this helps` as its data.
  Preserve the quotation; its surface resemblance to chatbot residue is irrelevant.
