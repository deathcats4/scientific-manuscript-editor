# Introduction

Use for Introduction drafting, diagnosis, or substantive restructuring. For a
local language edit, preserve a sound existing architecture.

## Scientific function

An Introduction should bring the intended reader from the relevant knowledge
state to the precise reason this study is needed. It should make clear:

- what scientific issue matters here;
- what is established;
- what remains unknown, unresolved, contradictory, or inaccessible;
- why that obstacle persists;
- what question, test, capability, or contribution this study supplies.

The gap is not a ceremonial sentence. It is the specific cognitive obstacle
that makes the study necessary.

## Diagnose before organizing

Determine which problem actually structures the study: an unknown phenomenon,
mechanism gap, causal uncertainty, competing explanations, literature tension,
methodological barrier, scale or condition boundary, untested prediction,
fragmented knowledge, or another concrete problem.

Then decide what the intended reader already knows, what must be established
before the study move makes sense, and what the Introduction should leave the
reader expecting from the paper.

## Organize for this problem

Use the scientific movement that best fits. A broad-to-specific or CARS-like
movement can work, but so can problem–solution, tension-driven,
hypothesis-driven, barrier-to-capability, anomaly-first, contribution-first, a
direct specialist opening, or a hybrid. These are examples, not slots.

Use only background needed to establish the question. Organize literature by
its relationship to the problem—agreement, boundary, conflict, missing test,
or enabling method—rather than paper by paper. State the study move at the
point where the reader can understand why it addresses the obstacle.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent a gap merely because an Introduction is expected to contain
  one.
- Do not claim novelty without a verified literature basis.
- Do not imply that prior work failed when it addressed a different object,
  scale, or condition.
- Do not preview results so extensively that the Introduction performs the
  Results or Discussion.
- Do not make the study objective broader than the evidence and analyses can
  answer.

## Completion check

The reader should be able to state the unresolved problem, why it remains
unresolved, what this study does about it, and the level of contribution the
paper can legitimately claim.
