# Terminology and consistency

Use this reference when writing or revising more than a single isolated
sentence, or whenever a passage contains a terminology conflict. The ledger is
the working source of truth for the supplied manuscript scope. It records what
the author has decided and makes unresolved choices visible; it does not give
the editor permission to rename scientific concepts.

## Build the ledger

Extract recurring terms from the supplied text and source material before
editing a section or full manuscript. Include:

- scientific objects, processes, minerals, materials, samples, stages, and
  named populations;
- methods, models, datasets, measurements, metrics, and variables;
- abbreviations, acronyms, symbols, mathematical notation, and units;
- terms defined by the paper or repeatedly used to carry its argument;
- figure, table, sample, and object names whose spelling or numbering must stay
  stable.

For each concept, record:

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Concept | The scientific thing or relationship the term denotes |
| Canonical form | Author-confirmed preferred Chinese or English term |
| Counterpart | Chinese–English mapping, if both languages are used |
| First-use form | Full name and abbreviation or symbol at first definition |
| Allowed contextual variants | A variant and the exact context in which it is allowed |
| Non-interchangeable terms | Similar terms that must remain distinct |
| Evidence/status | Source location and whether the author has confirmed it |

Do not treat spelling variants as separate concepts until their contexts have
been inspected. Conversely, do not merge terms merely because they look
similar: differences in object, process, stage, scale, mineral generation,
evidence level, or causal role may be scientifically meaningful.

## Author decisions

For a section or full-manuscript task, show a compact ledger or conflict list
when the first terminology choices affect the text. Separate:

- a confirmed canonical term, which can be enforced;
- a clear mechanical deviation from a confirmed term, which can be corrected
  directly and grouped in the change summary;
- an unresolved synonym or naming choice, which requires the author's decision;
- a possible conceptual collision, which requires reporting the contexts and
  asking whether the terms should be merged or kept distinct.

Do not choose a canonical term solely because it occurs most often. Frequency
can help formulate a recommendation, but the author's scientific usage and
decision control the ledger. Do not invent a missing term or silently resolve a
translation that could change the concept.

## Contextual variants

An allowed contextual variant is a deliberate form that denotes the same
ledger concept under a stated condition. Typical examples include:

- a full name at first use and an abbreviation thereafter;
- an unabbreviated term in running prose and a defined abbreviation in a
  figure, table, or space-limited label;
- a grammatical singular/plural or inflected form when the concept remains the
  same;
- a parent term in one context and a clearly defined display label, provided
  the mapping is explicit;
- a term for a mineral and a term for its aggregate only when the author has
  confirmed that the distinction is intentional and the contexts are clear.

Record the condition, scope, and mapping for every allowed variant. A variant
without a stated condition is unresolved drift. If a difference changes the
object, hierarchy, process, stage, scale, generation, or evidence role, record
separate concepts and do not list the forms as interchangeable variants.

## Enforce and sweep

After the author confirms the ledger, use its canonical forms in new prose and
preserve only the recorded contextual variants. Check the whole supplied scope,
including headings, abstract, main text, captions, tables, supplementary text,
and cross-references when available. Check that:

- abbreviations are defined once before use and then used consistently;
- Chinese–English mappings remain stable;
- symbols, units, capitalization, hyphenation, and numbering follow the
  confirmed form;
- one concept does not drift across sections, and one term is not silently used
  for two concepts;
- later author renaming updates every occurrence in the supplied scope and the
  ledger.

Inspect contexts before changing any flagged occurrence. Report unresolved
collisions to the author and pause only the affected text. Mechanical fixes can
be applied directly and listed together in the change summary. The final check
must distinguish a true terminology error from an intentional contextual
variant and from a scientific distinction.

For a narrow passage, keep this check light and report only conflicts that
affect the passage or its immediate terminology context. For a full manuscript
or a multi-round revision, run a retrospective sweep after content changes and
repeat it until no new terminology drift is found.
